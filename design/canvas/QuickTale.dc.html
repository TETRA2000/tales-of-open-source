The Night Watch

How one thread keeps thousands of connections moving — without ever waiting at a door.

●○○ Newcomer 4 min read nodejs/node @ [SHA]

Node.js runs your JavaScript on a single thread. Only one line of your code executes at any moment. And yet one Node server can hold thousands of open connections at once. The trick is not more threads — it is a loop that never stops making rounds.

Meet the watchman

Picture a night watchman walking a ring of six doors. On every lap he opens each door in the same order, deals with whatever is waiting behind it, and moves on. If a door is empty, he walks past. He never stands at a door waiting for something to arrive — except at one door, and only when there is nothing else to do.

The express lane between doors

There is one more rule. Every time the watchman finishes a single task, he checks a small tray in his pocket before touching the next one. Two kinds of notes go in that tray: process.nextTick callbacks first, then resolved Promise callbacks. The tray is always emptied completely — which is why a runaway chain of nextTick calls can keep him from ever reaching the next door.

The real function also runs due timers once more at the end of each lap, and caps how long uv__io_poll may block so timers are not missed.

Remember three things
  1. One thread runs your code; libuv's loop decides which callback runs next.
  2. Each lap visits six phases in a fixed order. Only poll may wait.
  3. nextTick and Promise callbacks jump the queue after every single callback.
